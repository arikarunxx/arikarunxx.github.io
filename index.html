The problem

Entries were made long after the fact, so a January transaction could first appear in Xero in December. Balances could not be relied on, and the back office team did not yet know how to use Xero.

What I did

I built a set of working papers in the style of an audit file: a trial balance tied to 20 account schedules, each with its supporting documents, plus a findings list of 18 issues with the adjustment for each. Then I trained the back office team until they could keep Xero current themselves.

The cleanup also showed where the manual work was. The ordering portal and payroll system below were built during the same engagement.

The problem

Orders came in by chat until a 16:00 cut off. Each one was typed into an invoice, recapped by hand for the harvest team, passed to packing, then corrected the next day once actual quantities were known. Back office staff came in on Sundays to finish the recap.

What I built

A personal order page for each customer that feeds Xero directly. Every order creates a draft invoice with the right item codes and division tracking. The recap updates live, so harvest has it at cut off, and packing slips print from the same data.

Customer orders→ Draft invoice in Xero→ Live recap at cut off→ Harvest and packing slips→ Quantity adjustment

The problem

The books were kept by hand and sales ran through two different POS systems. Recipe costs left out the loss in preparation and cooking, so the real cost of each dish was understated and selling prices were set too low.

What I did

I initiated the move to Xero and made the case to the owner. After comparing POS options, I recommended ESB for its detailed restaurant reporting and consolidated Abacus and Moka into it. Then I built a COGS working paper that costs every recipe including process loss, so the right selling price for each item became visible. I also built a Google Sheet that compares weekly price lists from four or more suppliers and generates a ready to send WhatsApp order for each one, so the purchasing decision and the order both happen in one step. On top of that I designed a weekly management report in Google Sheets sent directly to the owner: cash flow, weekly income and expenditure, the biggest spend lines broken down by payee, weekly COGS, process loss, and stock variance with a specific focus on alcohol.

The problem

Statements from several banks, each laid out differently, had to be read, checked against their own balances, and scanned line by line for items that need an explanation.

What I built

A single offline file that works with any bank's PDF statement. BCA, BRI, Mandiri, and BNI have dedicated readers, other banks are read by detecting their columns, and a new layout only needs its columns mapped once before it is saved for that bank.

It runs 40 checks on three levels. Each transaction is tested against 24 red flags graded High, Medium, or Info, such as round trips with a third party, period end reversals, and amounts just below a threshold. Each statement goes through 9 reconciliation checks. Each PDF file gets 7 authenticity checks, for example whether it was produced by a bank system or saved from general software. Reviewers mark every flag with a status and a note under their name, and everything exports to Excel in seven sheets.

PDF statements→ Read and reconciled→ Flags for review→ Reviewer notes→ Excel workpaper

Browser based tool, built in HTML and JavaScript · pdf.js to read the PDF statements · SheetJS to export to Excel · No install, runs offline · Any bank with a text based PDF statement
